1 Executive Summary

Needs Work

Main Street Contractors has a solid technical foundation — HTTPS, mobile-friendly markup, LocalBusiness and GeneralContractor schema, and a clean URL structure with dedicated service-area pages for Indianapolis, Carmel, Fishers, and parts of Ohio. Title tags and meta descriptions are present on every page reviewed, and image alt text is in place across the homepage. That puts you ahead of a lot of small-contractor sites in the Indianapolis market.

The biggest opportunities are around content depth and proof. There is no dedicated portfolio or project gallery indexed on the site, no blog or educational content for homeowners researching remodels, no FAQ schema to win answer-box placements, and the service-area pages — while present — could be expanded with neighborhood-specific signals (Broad Ripple, Meridian-Kessler, Geist, Zionsville). A handful of meta and schema tweaks plus a real portfolio section would meaningfully lift the site's standing for high-intent local searches.

6 Content Gap Recommendations

Topics and content types your competitors cover that you're missing.

Project portfolio with case studies High Priority

Homeowners shortlisting a contractor want to see real work. A dedicated portfolio with before/after photos, project scope, timeline, and budget range builds credibility and earns long-tail visibility (e.g. 'mid-century kitchen remodel Indianapolis').

Recommended format: Gallery page + individual case-study sub-pages with ImageObject schema

Per-service landing pages High Priority

Right now Kitchen, Bath, Insurance Repair, and Commercial are sub-sections of one Services page. Splitting them into standalone pages lets each rank for its own keyword cluster.

Recommended format: Individual /services/[name] pages with FAQ schema and a project gallery slice

Neighborhood-specific service area pages Medium Priority

You already have 9 city-level pages — extending to high-intent Indy neighborhoods (Broad Ripple, Meridian-Kessler, Geist, Zionsville, Westfield) would capture hyper-local searches with low competition.

Recommended format: Neighborhood landing pages with embedded map, project examples, and local landmarks

Cost guides and remodeling FAQs Medium Priority

'Cost to remodel kitchen Indianapolis' and similar long-tail queries have steady volume and almost no local competition. A cost guide also doubles as a sales tool the team can send to leads.

Recommended format: Long-form blog posts with FAQ schema and price-range tables

Reviews and testimonials surfaced on-site Medium Priority

Reviews appear to live primarily on Google. Embedding aggregated review schema on the homepage helps ranking, while testimonial pages with photos drive conversion.

Recommended format: AggregateRating schema in homepage LocalBusiness + dedicated /reviews page

Process and 'what to expect' content Low Priority

First-time remodel customers are anxious about cost, timeline, and disruption. A 'How we work' page with phases, timelines, and FAQs reduces friction and builds trust before the first call.

Recommended format: Single page with milestone graphic, FAQs, and embedded video walkthrough
