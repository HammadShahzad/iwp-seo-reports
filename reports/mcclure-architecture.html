1 Executive Summary
52/100
Critical

McCLURE architecture has a beautifully restrained, design-forward website that reflects the firm's classical residential work — the imagery is gorgeous and the project library is genuinely impressive. The bones are solid: HTTPS is in place, canonical URLs are set, meta descriptions exist on most pages, and each project lives on its own indexable URL, which is more than most boutique architecture firms can say.

The big opportunity is that the site reads as a portfolio to humans but reads as almost nothing to Google. Image alt text is auto-generated AI captions like "A black and white photo of an old house" instead of descriptive captions a search engine could use, project pages have stunning photos but zero accompanying narrative text, and the word "Memphis" doesn't appear in the homepage title, H1, or meta description — meaning the site barely competes for searches like "custom home architect Memphis" despite the firm being uniquely qualified to win them.

6 Content Gap Recommendations

Topics and content types your competitors cover that you're missing.

Individual project narratives High Priority

Project pages are currently photo-only galleries. Adding 250–400 words per project with location, design intent, materials, and client story would create real indexable content for searches like "Georgian home architect" or "Belgian farmhouse design".

Recommended format: Long-form project case studies on each portfolio page

Memphis-focused service pages High Priority

There is no page targeting "custom home architect Memphis" or "Memphis residential architect" — the most valuable local commercial searches for the firm.

Recommended format: Dedicated service landing page with Memphis FAQs, process, and project examples

Style guides for traditional architecture Medium Priority

Buyers researching their dream home search by style — Colonial Revival, French Provincial, Georgian. The firm has the expertise to own these informational searches and turn them into leads.

Recommended format: Short educational articles, one per architectural style

FAQ — cost, timeline, working with an architect Medium Priority

"How much does a custom home architect cost" and "do I need an architect for a remodel" are high-volume informational queries with strong commercial intent for luxury residential firms.

Recommended format: FAQ page or short blog series with transparent ranges

Process page Medium Priority

Design Services explains services but not the client journey. A dedicated process page (Discovery → Schematic → DD → CD → CA) reassures high-net-worth clients evaluating boutique firms.

Recommended format: Visual step-by-step process page

Press detail pages Low Priority

The Press section likely lists publications but each mention could be its own indexable page citing the source and project — small SEO + reputation wins.

Recommended format: Individual press feature pages with backlinks to original publications
