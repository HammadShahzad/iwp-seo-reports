1 Executive Summary

Needs Work

Dinnes Studio has a clean, well-positioned site with strong on-page foundations — clear title tags, meta descriptions on every page, and an intentional focus on Oklahoma City neighborhoods like Nichols Hills, Edmond, Midtown, and the Plaza District. The Squarespace base is doing a lot of right things out of the box, and your messaging around context-driven, durable design comes through clearly.

Where there's room to grow is in the depth of your project pages, image accessibility (most portfolio photos are missing descriptive alt text), and local SEO signals like LocalBusiness schema and a Google Business Profile. The portfolio is the heart of your site — making each project page individually search-friendly is where the biggest gains live for a firm at your stage.

6 Content Gap Recommendations

Topics and content types your competitors cover that you're missing.

Neighborhood-specific landing pages (Nichols Hills, Edmond, Midtown, Plaza District) High Priority

You already concentrate work in specific neighborhoods — dedicated pages capture hyperlocal searches competitors aren't actively targeting

Recommended format: One page per neighborhood with 400-600 words, embedded relevant projects, and local context

Detailed project case studies High Priority

Architecture clients deeply research before reaching out — substantive project pages signal expertise and rank for project-type searches

Recommended format: Per-project narrative covering client brief, site constraints, design moves, materials, and outcomes, plus high-quality captioned imagery

Residential design process page High Priority

High-end residential clients want to understand what working with you actually looks like — process content reduces friction and ranks for 'custom home process' searches

Recommended format: Step-by-step guide from initial consultation through construction administration

Cost and timeline guides Medium Priority

Educational top-of-funnel content captures clients still in research mode — earns trust and links back to your services

Recommended format: Long-form Journal posts (1500+ words) with charts, FAQs, and clear next steps

Adaptive reuse / historic preservation positioning Medium Priority

Mezzo Bakeshop and similar work could anchor a strong niche page — adaptive reuse is a high-intent search category with limited local competition

Recommended format: Dedicated service page tying together past projects, materials philosophy, and process

Multi-family / developer-focused page Medium Priority

Plaza 14, The Juniper, Flats on Francis show real multi-family chops — a developer-targeted page separates this from the residential audience

Recommended format: Standalone service page with case studies, unit counts, and developer-focused language

7 Prioritized Action Plan
Quick Wins do this week — under 2 hours each
  1. Add descriptive alt text to every portfolio image — Google can't 'see' your architecture without it, and this is a 1-2 hour fix sitewide
  2. Add a strong H1 to the homepage that names your services and city (e.g. 'Oklahoma City Residential & Commercial Architecture')
  3. Create or verify your Google Business Profile and link it from the site footer with full NAP
  4. Add Architect or LocalBusiness JSON-LD schema to the homepage (Squarespace supports this via code injection)
  5. Compress and convert hero images to WebP — should noticeably improve mobile Core Web Vitals
  6. Add internal links from project pages to relevant service or neighborhood pages once those exist
  7. Expand the About page to 500+ words with team bios and design philosophy — currently underperforming for 'okc architecture firm' searches
Strategic Investments plan for this quarter
  1. Build out neighborhood-specific landing pages for Nichols Hills, East Edmond, Midtown, and Plaza District — likely the single highest-ROI SEO move for a firm at your stage
  2. Rewrite every project page with 250-400 words of narrative context, captioned photography, and links to related work — turns the portfolio into a search asset
  3. Publish a consistent Journal cadence (1-2 substantive posts per month) targeting buyer-intent keywords like 'how much does an architect cost in OK' and 'do I need an architect for a custom home'
  4. Develop dedicated service pages for residential, multi-family, and boutique commercial — each with its own keyword focus and project showcase
  5. Build a press / awards page and actively pursue local design feature placements (405 Magazine, Oklahoma Today, AIA Oklahoma) for backlinks and domain authority
  6. Set up structured review collection (Google Business Profile, Houzz) — the testimonials on your homepage are excellent but aren't currently earning local SEO weight
